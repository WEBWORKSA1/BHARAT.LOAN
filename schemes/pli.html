PLI Scheme: Production Linked Incentive across 14 sectors

Production Linked Incentive · Government of India · ~₹1.97 lakh crore total approved outlay (US$25B+)

The Production Linked Incentive scheme is the central government's flagship manufacturing policy — a sustained 4 to 10% cash incentive on incremental sales over a base year, paid for 5-6 years, designed to attract large-scale investment, build domestic supply chains, and make India a global manufacturing hub.

PLI at a glance

If you are a manufacturer (Indian or foreign-owned) willing to commit significant fresh investment in any of 14 approved sectors, PLI pays you a percentage of your incremental sales above a baseline year, every year for 5-6 years. It rewards scale-up, not setup — you have to actually grow sales.

What you can get
A cash incentive of 4-10% on your incremental sales above a base year, paid for 5-6 years. The base year, incentive slab and committed investment threshold differ by sector. Combined outlay across all 14 sectors is approximately ₹1.97 lakh crore (US$25B+).
PLI is an output-linked incentive, not a capital subsidy. You get paid only on sales you actually achieve above the baseline. Underperformance forfeits the incentive. Designed to crowd in serious manufacturing investment, not subsidy-tourism.

Eligibility (general framework)

  • Manufacturer (Indian or foreign-owned) of products in one of the 14 notified sectors
  • Committed fresh investment meeting the sector-specific threshold (ranges from ₹10 cr for drones to ₹1,000+ cr for ACC battery)
  • Selected through ministry-led application/competitive selection windows (windows are sector-specific and time-bound)
  • Capacity to achieve and document incremental sales over the notified base year
  • Compliance with sector-specific value-addition and domestic-sourcing requirements

How to apply

  1. Identify the sector you fit (different ministry, different criteria, different application portal)
  2. Confirm the sector's application window is open (PLI windows are time-bound, not perpetual)
  3. Prepare a detailed project report with committed investment, technology pathway, value addition plan and incremental sales projections
  4. Apply through the respective ministry's PLI portal / nodal agency
  5. Once selected, the incentive is claimed annually based on audited incremental sales achievement

Frequently asked

What is the PLI scheme?
The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) is a central government scheme that pays manufacturers 4-10% cash incentive on incremental sales over a base year, sustained for 5-6 years. Total approved outlay across 14 sectors is approximately ₹1.97 lakh crore (US$25B+).
Which sectors are covered under PLI?
14 sectors: mobile manufacturing, IT hardware, telecom, pharmaceuticals, bulk drugs, medical devices, white goods, food processing, textile products, specialty steel, auto components, advanced chemistry cells, solar PV modules, and drones.
Who is eligible for PLI?
Sector-specific, but generally requires a minimum committed investment ranging from ₹10 crore (drones) up to ₹1,000+ crore (ACC battery). Both Indian and foreign-owned manufacturers can apply.
How does PLI differ from capital subsidies?
PLI is output-linked — you get paid only on sales you actually achieve above the baseline. Capital subsidies (PMEGP, CLCSS) reduce your investment cost up-front. PLI rewards scale-up and execution, not setup.
Can MSMEs apply for PLI?
In theory, yes — PLI doesn't bar MSMEs. In practice, the minimum committed investment thresholds (₹10 crore minimum for the smallest sector) put most micro and small enterprises out of reach. PLI is primarily a large-investor scheme.
