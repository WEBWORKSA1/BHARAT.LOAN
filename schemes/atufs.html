ATUFS: Amended Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme

Amended Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme · Notified 2016, last amended 2022 · Ministry of Textiles · ₹17,822 crore approved outlay

India's central textile-machinery capex scheme — a one-time Capital Investment Subsidy of 10-15% on new benchmarked textile machinery, credit-linked through a term loan, with the strongest 15% slab for garmenting, technical textiles and made-ups.

Quick eligibility read

Textile manufacturer (Company / LLP / MSME) investing in new benchmarked machinery from the Textile Commissioner's approved list, financed through a bank term loan, in one of the covered segments.

What you can get
A one-time Capital Investment Subsidy (CIS) on the cost of new benchmarked textile machinery — 15% (capped at ₹30 crore) for garmenting, technical textiles and made-ups; 10% (capped at ₹20 crore) for weaving (apparel fabric), processing of fibres/yarns/fabrics/garments/made-ups, jute, silk and handloom. Subsidy is credit-linked: the machinery must be financed through a term loan from a notified lending agency. Accessories, attachments and sample machines up to 20% of the machine cost are also eligible. Released in installments after Joint Inspection Team (JIT) certification.
Notified 2016 by the Ministry of Textiles (replacing the earlier RR-TUFS), with a ₹17,822 crore approved outlay and successive extensions. The scheme was further amended in 2022 to improve accessibility. ATUFS remains operational through the iTUFS portal (i-tufs.txcindia.gov.in) as of mid-2026; a PLI-style successor (Textile Technological Development Scheme, TTDS) is under discussion but the current scheme remains the active route for textile capex subsidies.

Who it's for

Textile manufacturers across India — Companies, LLPs and MSMEs — investing in new benchmarked machinery for garmenting, technical textiles, made-ups, weaving (apparel fabric), processing, knitting, jute, silk, handloom and embroidery. Both new (greenfield) and existing (brownfield) units are eligible; existing units who previously claimed RR-TUFS can claim only the remaining cap under ATUFS.

Eligibility

  • Registered entity — Company (Companies Act), LLP, or MSME with Udyam Registration
  • Acknowledgement of Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM) or registration with the respective State Industries Directorate
  • Investment in new benchmarked machinery from the Textile Commissioner's eligible machinery list (no second-hand)
  • Mandatory term loan from a notified lending agency (scheme is credit-linked)
  • Pre-installation UID generated on the iTUFS portal before placing machinery orders
  • Operates in a covered textile segment (spinning of cotton / synthetic filament yarn excluded)
  • If prior RR-TUFS beneficiary, only the residual subsidy cap is available under ATUFS

How to apply

  1. Confirm your textile segment and the eligibility of the specific machinery against the current Textile Commissioner's benchmarked list (refreshed April 1st annually)
  2. Arrange a term loan from a notified lending agency for the machinery investment
  3. Before placing machinery orders, register on the iTUFS portal (i-tufs.txcindia.gov.in) and obtain a Unique Identification Number (UID) per machine
  4. Place orders against the UID; install and commission the machinery
  5. Joint Inspection Team (JIT) — comprising Textile Commissioner, lending bank and machinery supplier — inspects and certifies
  6. Claim the Capital Investment Subsidy online; released in installments (typically over multiple years) against the term loan

Frequently asked

What does ATUFS actually pay?
A one-time Capital Investment Subsidy on the cost of new benchmarked textile machinery — 15% (capped at ₹30 crore) for garmenting, technical textiles and made-ups; 10% (capped at ₹20 crore) for weaving (apparel fabric), processing, jute, silk and handloom. The subsidy is credit-linked: you must finance the machinery through a term loan.
Can I claim ATUFS on second-hand machinery?
No — only new benchmarked machinery from notified manufacturers or their authorised agents qualifies. The Textile Commissioner publishes the eligible-machinery list afresh every April 1st.
Does ATUFS stack with state textile policies?
Yes — ATUFS is central and stacks with the Gujarat Textile Policy 2024 (10-35% capital + 5-7% interest subsidy), UP Textile & Garmenting Policy 2022, and similar state-level packages. Combined recovery on garmenting / made-ups capex routinely reaches 40-50% once stacked correctly.
What is replacing ATUFS?
The Ministry of Textiles has signalled it intends to replace ATUFS with a PLI-style Textile Technological Development Scheme (TTDS), but ATUFS remains operational through the iTUFS portal as of mid-2026. Industry — particularly the Surat belt — has pushed back, requesting extension of ATUFS instead. The current scheme remains the route for textile capex subsidies until formally replaced.
What's the most common application mistake?
Placing machinery orders before generating the iTUFS UID. The UID must exist before procurement — orders placed without a prior UID are not eligible. Second most common: ordering machinery not on the benchmarked list (the list is product- and model-specific, not generic).
