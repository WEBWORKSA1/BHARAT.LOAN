How to get a business loan without collateral in India

The collateral-free routes that actually work in 2026 — Mudra, CGTMSE, PMEGP, Stand-Up India and state schemes — and how to choose between them.

By the BHARAT.LOAN research desk · Reviewed by our advisory desk · Last reviewed 25 Nov 2026

Short answer: start with Mudra if you need up to ₹20 lakh, ask your bank to process a bigger loan under CGTMSE (up to ₹10 crore), and if you are starting a new unit check PMEGP or your state’s margin-money scheme first — the subsidy is money you don’t repay.

Why banks ask for collateral — and how schemes remove it

A bank asks for collateral because it carries the full loss if a borrower defaults. Government credit schemes take that risk off the bank: either a government trust guarantees most of the loan (CGTMSE), or the loan is refinanced and monitored through a public framework (Mudra), or the government contributes part of the project cost as a subsidy (PMEGP, CMEGP, ODOP). That is why, under these schemes, banks are told not to insist on collateral or third-party guarantees.

The main collateral-free routes

1. PM Mudra Yojana — up to ₹20 lakh

Mudra loans come in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who have repaid an earlier Tarun loan). They are offered by banks, NBFCs and microfinance institutions for non-farm income-generating activities. Full Mudra guide →

2. CGTMSE — up to ₹10 crore

CGTMSE is not a loan; it is a guarantee behind your bank loan. Eligible micro and small enterprises can borrow without collateral, and the trust guarantees a large share of the bank’s risk for a small annual fee. Ask your branch to process your term loan or working capital “under CGTMSE”. Full CGTMSE guide →

3. PMEGP and state margin-money schemes — subsidy plus loan

For a brand-new unit, PMEGP gives a 15–35% subsidy on projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (service); loans up to ₹10 lakh under PMEGP generally do not need collateral, and higher amounts are often covered by CGTMSE. States run similar schemes: CMEGP in Maharashtra, CMYSY and ODOP in Uttar Pradesh.

4. Stand-Up India — ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore

For SC, ST and women entrepreneurs starting a greenfield enterprise, Stand-Up India offers a composite loan of ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore, backed by a dedicated credit guarantee fund.

5. Special state routes

Uttar Pradesh’s CM-YUVA gives youth aged 21–40 a 0% interest, collateral-free loan up to ₹5 lakh. In Maharashtra, the Annasaheb Patil scheme reimburses interest on eligible business loans.

Which one should you try first?

  • Starting a new unit, need under ₹50 lakh: PMEGP or your state margin-money scheme first (you keep the subsidy), with CGTMSE cover for the loan.
  • Existing small business, need under ₹20 lakh: Mudra.
  • Bigger working capital or term loan: a regular MSME loan under CGTMSE.
  • SC/ST or woman founder, ₹10 lakh–₹1 crore: Stand-Up India.
  • Startup with a tech or innovation angle: DPIIT recognition first, then the Seed Fund and the startup credit guarantee.

How to improve your approval odds

  1. Register on Udyam (free) before you apply.
  2. Write a realistic project report — sales, costs, margins and a repayment plan.
  3. Keep 6–12 months of clean bank statements; avoid cheque bounces.
  4. Check your credit score and fix errors before applying.
  5. Apply at a public sector bank branch that handles scheme loans regularly, and ask for the MSME nodal officer.

Red flags

Nobody can “guarantee” a government-scheme loan. Don’t pay agents for approvals, don’t share OTPs, and apply only on official portals or directly at the bank.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a business loan without collateral in India?
Yes. Mudra loans up to ₹20 lakh and CGTMSE-guaranteed MSME loans up to ₹10 crore don’t require collateral or third-party guarantees, and PMEGP, Stand-Up India and several state schemes also offer collateral-free routes.
What is the easiest collateral-free loan for a small business?
For small amounts, a Mudra loan (Shishu, Kishore or Tarun) from a bank or NBFC is usually the simplest. For a new unit, a PMEGP or state margin-money loan adds a subsidy you don’t have to repay.
Does CGTMSE cost anything?
Yes, the lender pays an annual guarantee fee, which is usually passed on to the borrower. It is generally much cheaper than the cost of pledging property.
Do I need a good credit score?
Banks check your credit history for every scheme loan. A clean repayment record and no recent defaults improve your chances considerably.
