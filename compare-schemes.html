How to read a scheme comparison

Indian government schemes for businesses fall into five archetypes, and they're not directly substitutable for each other.

1. Credit guarantees

CGTMSE covers the lender against default — the lender takes lower collateral risk and you get a loan you wouldn't otherwise qualify for. The interest rate is regular market rate; the value is unlocking access, not reducing cost.

2. Margin-money subsidies

PMEGP and most state subsidies reduce the portion of the project you have to repay. The bank disburses the full loan; the subsidy is adjusted against your outstanding after a lock-in period (typically three years). Net effect: smaller principal, smaller EMI.

3. Capital subsidies

CLCSS pays a fixed percent of your plant-and-machinery cost as a one-time grant, typically after commissioning and verification. Restricted to notified sub-sectors and to existing units doing tech upgrade — not new units.

4. Concessional loans

Mudra, MSME 59-Min, SIDBI Direct offer better-than-market loan terms (lower rates, faster turnaround, collateral relaxation) without changing the principal. The value is in interest savings and time-to-money, not in subsidy.

5. Grants & tax benefits

SISFS gives non-dilutive grants; DPIIT 80-IAC exempts startup income tax for 3 of 10 years. Both require DPIIT recognition. They don't compete with credit schemes — they stack alongside.

The right comparison question

Not "which scheme is best." The schemes target different problems. The right question is: given my stage, sector, location and ticket size, what's the best stack of schemes I can claim simultaneously? That's what the Subsidy Estimator answers, and what an advisor confirms before filing.

Comparison questions, answered

What's the actual difference between CGTMSE and PMEGP?
CGTMSE is a credit guarantee covering collateral-free MSME loans up to ₹10 Cr at standard bank rates. PMEGP is a margin-money subsidy of 15–35% of project cost for new manufacturing (cap ₹25L) and service units (cap ₹10L). CGTMSE makes loans accessible without collateral; PMEGP reduces the loan amount you have to repay. They cannot be combined for the same unit.
Should I take PMEGP or Mudra for a new business?
For new manufacturing or service projects below ₹25L, PMEGP wins because the 15–35% subsidy is permanent free money. For micro-business projects below ₹20L that don't fit PMEGP categories or where you need speed, Mudra Tarun is collateral-free, faster (15–45 days), and stacks with most other schemes. Mudra is a loan; PMEGP is a loan + subsidy.
Can I combine multiple government schemes?
Some can stack, some cannot. PMEGP cannot be combined with CGTMSE, CLCSS or ODOP-UP for the same unit. Mudra and MSME 59-Minute loans generally stack with each other and with most schemes. DPIIT recognition and Section 80-IAC tax benefit stack with everything since they're recognition and tax, not subsidies. Always confirm with the lending agency before assuming a stack.
Which scheme approves the fastest?
MSME Loan in 59 Minutes gives in-principle approval within 59 minutes and disburses in 7–15 days for established MSMEs with GST history. Mudra typically disburses in 15–45 days. CGTMSE 45–90 days. PMEGP and CLCSS 90–180 days. State subsidies 60–180 days.
Do these schemes require collateral?
CGTMSE and Mudra are explicitly collateral-free. SISFS and DPIIT 80-IAC are not loans so collateral isn't applicable. PMEGP and CLCSS depend on the lending bank — most banks route these through CGTMSE to avoid collateral. MSME 59-Minute and SIDBI Direct typically require collateral unless paired with CGTMSE.
What is CLCSS and how is it different from PMEGP?
CLCSS is a 15% capital subsidy on plant and machinery for technology upgrade in existing manufacturing MSMEs in 51 notified sub-sectors. PMEGP is 15–35% margin-money subsidy for new units across most sectors. CLCSS is upgrade-only; PMEGP is new-unit-only. Different units of the same business can sometimes claim both at different times.
